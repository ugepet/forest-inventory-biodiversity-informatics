# Project Scope

## 1. Working Title

Forest Inventory and Biodiversity Informatics: A Reproducible Research Programme.

## 2. Background

Forest inventories provide information about tree species composition, abundance, and geographic occurrence. However, legacy inventory datasets may contain inconsistent scientific names, incomplete metadata, coordinate errors, and variable recording practices.

This project will investigate both the ecological information contained in a georeferenced forest inventory and the informatics methods required to improve its quality, interoperability, and reproducibility.

## 3. Overall Aim

To develop a reproducible workflow for validating, harmonizing, and analysing a georeferenced forest inventory, producing defensible ecological findings and evaluating relevant biodiversity-informatics methods.

## 4. Specific Objectives

1. Audit the workbook structure, sampling design, metadata, and provenance.
2. Assess missingness, duplication, coordinate validity, taxonomic inconsistencies, and other data-quality issues.
3. Develop a documented workflow for taxonomic harmonization and geospatial validation.
4. Establish a versioned, quality-controlled master dataset.
5. Evaluate ecological patterns supported by the inventory and its sampling design.
6. Investigate the suitability of biodiversity standards and interoperable data representations.
7. Assess opportunities for spatial modelling and machine learning using appropriate supplementary data.
8. Develop distinct, reproducible analyses for scientifically justified publications.

## 5. Research Domains

### Ecology and Forestry

- Floristic composition and diversity.
- Species abundance and community structure.
- Spatial patterns and community differentiation.
- Disturbance-related patterns, if sampling metadata support valid comparisons.
- Conservation relevance.
- Overstorey and ground-flora relationships, where observations permit.

### Computer Science and Informatics

- Taxonomic name matching and reconciliation.
- Data-quality metrics and validation.
- Geospatial quality assurance.
- Biodiversity data interoperability.
- Reproducible computational workflows.
- GeoAI and explainable machine learning, subject to data sufficiency.

## 6. Scope Boundaries

The initial study is based on the supplied inventory workbook.

The following must not be assumed before verification:

- The exact study-site identity and administrative boundaries.
- The dimensions and independence of inventory plots.
- The sampling protocol and sampling effort.
- The completeness of individual tree records.
- The availability of reliable abundance or diameter measurements.
- The completeness and comparability of ground-flora observations.
- The accuracy of conservation-status labels.
- The suitability of the dataset for species distribution modelling.

Any additional environmental, climatic, remote-sensing, or taxonomic reference data will be documented separately.

## 7. Research Integrity

The raw source file will remain unchanged. All cleaning decisions, exclusions, corrections, transformations, and analytical parameters will be documented.

Uncertain taxonomic identifications and coordinate anomalies will be flagged for review rather than silently corrected.

Results will be reported only after the corresponding analyses and quality-control checks have been completed.

## 8. Expected Outputs

- A documented data audit.
- A reproducible data-cleaning and validation workflow.
- A quality-controlled master inventory, subject to data permissions.
- Ecological and informatics analytical outputs.
- Reusable code, figures, and tables.
- Scientifically distinct manuscripts.
- Appropriate public research materials, subject to data governance and licensing.

## 9. Initial Project Stage

The project is currently in the setup and data-audit preparation stage. No final analytical results have been established.
